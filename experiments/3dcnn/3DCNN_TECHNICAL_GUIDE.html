1. System overview

The model operates on dense cropped MNI volumes. It never reduces the image to atlas-parcel features before the CNN.

Stage 1
volume → AE latent → reconstruction
→
Stage 3
brain ↔ text contrastive alignment
→
Stage 4
text → AE latent → generated volume

Stage 1 learns a spatial representation without text. Stage 3 initializes its brain encoder from Stage 1 and aligns that encoder with a SPECTER2 text projection through symmetric InfoNCE. Stage 4 learns a new text-to-raw-AE-latent projection while freezing the exact autoencoder used by the branch.

Atlas-free inputOne-channel MNI152 4 mm brain crop, directly processed by Conv3D.
Controlled branchesMixed baseline versus matching domain specialization inside each domain.
Evaluation-only testValidation selects checkpoints; held-out test data never changes training.

The training surface comprises a shared atlas_free_cnn_volumes.pt tensor plus train.jsonl, val.jsonl, and test.jsonl. Each JSONL row identifies a volume by tensor_path and tensor_index, carries source and map metadata, and lists one or more positive texts.

The original split builder used seed 42 and stratified by the full source string before combining and shuffling the groups. Published split files are now treated as immutable inputs and fingerprinted before training. Stage 1A samples them naturally; no inverse-frequency or source-balanced sampler is active.

3.1 PubMed coordinate maps

  1. Group MNI coordinates by PMID and place unique peaks as impulses on a 4 mm MNI brain mask.
  2. Smooth with a 9 mm FWHM Gaussian: 3.822 mm sigma, or 0.9555 voxels at 4 mm.
  3. Apply the brain mask and crop the full [46,55,46] grid with slices [5:41,5:50,1:39].
  4. Replace NaN/Inf by zero, clamp negative values, and divide each nonempty map by its maximum.
  5. Store the resulting [36,45,38] map as float16.

Possible text positives include paper title, wiki-style summary, and filtered MeSH anatomical-region, cognitive-construct, or disorder labels. Summary-style text outranks title-only text for the primary target.

Each non-background label or 4D component becomes its own example. Binary data uses nearest-neighbor resampling; continuous/probabilistic data uses continuous interpolation. Labels are turned into human-readable term/definition pairs, then every image is packed into the common 4 mm crop and normalized to [0,1].

3.3 NeuroVault statistical maps

  1. Accept volumetric NIfTI images and reject surface/CIFTI-like formats or malformed shapes.
  2. Resample to the 4 mm MNI mask—nearest for binary maps, continuous otherwise.
  3. Clean NaN/Inf, record negative-value presence, retain the positive part, and apply the mask.
  4. Crop to [36,45,38] and reject positive nonzero fraction below 0.001.
  5. Robustly scale positive values between the 1st and 99.5th percentiles, then clip to [0,1].
  6. Keep strong-quality rows only and cap accepted rows at 50 per collection.

Quality scoring rewards names, descriptions, task/contrast labels, DOI/PMID, collection descriptions, and Cognitive Atlas fields. It penalizes missing metadata, failed resampling, nearly empty images, suspicious thresholding, odd shapes, and low-quality text. Text candidates come from image descriptions, tasks/contrasts, collection metadata, and publication titles.

4. Model architecture and parameter structure

4.1 Base-64 finalized encoder

Every block is Conv3d(3×3×3, padding=1) → GroupNorm (up to 8 groups) → GELU → MaxPool3d(2).

LayerOutput
Input[B,1,36,45,38]
Block 1, 64 channels[B,64,18,22,19]
Block 2, 128 channels[B,128,9,11,9]
Block 3, 256 channels[B,256,4,5,4]
Block 4, 512 channels[B,512,2,2,2]
Adaptive average pool[B,512,1,1,1]
Flatten, dropout 0.1, linear[B,384]

4.2 Decoder

A linear layer expands the 384-d latent into a [B,512,3,3,3] seed. Four kernel-2, stride-2 ConvTranspose3D blocks upsample while reducing channels to 64. A final 3×3 Conv3D produces one channel; trilinear interpolation makes the result exactly [36,45,38].

Output semantics: the decoder has no sigmoid. Raw outputs feed the training loss. Evaluation clamps predictions to [0,1].

8.2 Stage 3 retrieval

Text→brain and brain→text directions report recall@1/5/10/50, MRR, median best-positive rank, and the full recall@k curve AUC across every k from 1 through N using normalized x-axis k/N. Random expected AUC is about 0.5; perfect retrieval is 1.0.

8.3 Stage 4 semantic evaluation

Generated maps are re-encoded by the matching frozen Stage 3 brain encoder and compared with its matching text projection. Reports cover raw and clamped maps, strict identity, same-text groups, publication/collection groups, and matched-versus-shuffled cosine. The stable primary semantic alias is clamped strict-map mean bidirectional normalized AUC.

Comparison rule: conclusions should be paired within domain—mixed Stage 1A versus matching Stage 1B—on equal split fingerprints. Cross-domain rankings are descriptive, not controlled specialization comparisons.

11. Known review caveats

  1. Notebook 1 effective AE loss: raw MSE, despite legacy sparse-loss arguments in the notebook command.
  2. Notebook 3→4 handoff: Notebook 4 uses locked AE resource loaders and builds its own validated manifest; it does not automatically pick up Notebook 3's newest registry.
  3. Hugging Face viewer: the viewer can fail schema auto-casting on heterogeneous raw JSONL rows. Notebook loading uses raw files and is unaffected.
  4. Image builders: the finalized image ingestion scripts were removed from the active tree. The public manifest/audit and repository history preserve the preparation provenance; the active tree retains the reproducible text-cache builder.
  5. Notebook 2: the ResNet48 multi-scale-attention experiment is retained separately but is outside this requested Notebook 1/3/4 path.
